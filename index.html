International Political Economy, AI, and Institutional Change

Anran (Ariel) Sheng

S. Rajaratnam School of International Studies (RSIS), Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore

I study how AI and digital transformation reshape political-economic institutions, with a focus on state-market relations, regulatory governance, industrial upgrading, and institutional adaptation in China and Asia. My work connects international political economy, technology governance, and institutional analysis to understand how firms, regulators, and public institutions respond to technological change under competition, geopolitical pressure, and shifting policy priorities.

State-market relations How policy, regulation, and market incentives shape AI adoption and industrial transformation.
Technology governance How public and private actors allocate accountability, risk, and decision authority around AI systems.
China and Asian political economy How institutional arrangements mediate digital strategy, development priorities, and cross-border pressures.

Research Agenda

Current focus: graduate research in International Political Economy, centered on AI, technology governance, institutional change, and China/Asia.
AI, Technology Governance, and Institutional Adaptation in China

My proposed research examines how AI adoption interacts with political-economic institutions, regulatory priorities, and development strategies. I am particularly interested in how technology policy changes incentives for firms and public organizations, and when digital transformation strengthens capacity, shifts risk, or creates new forms of institutional dependence.

Research Interests

  • Political economy of AI adoption under competition, regulation, and state development priorities.
  • Technology governance, including accountability, risk allocation, and public-private coordination.
  • China and Asian comparative political economy, especially digital strategy and institutional adaptation.

Methods and Data Strategy

  • Quantitative strategy: panel-data analysis and quasi-experimental logic when identification allows.
  • Text-based analysis: policy documents, annual reports, public communications, and regulatory materials.
  • Qualitative strategy: interviews and process tracing to identify mechanisms.
  • Empirical contexts: Chinese firms and public institutions, with comparative extensions across Asia.

Working Papers in Development

AI Adoption Under Institutional Pressure: Evidence from China's Political Economy

Governance by Design: Accountability and Risk in AI-Enabled Public and Private Institutions

This study examines how AI-enabled workflow redesign changes accountability structures, risk allocation, and the boundary between administrative, managerial, and system discretion.
